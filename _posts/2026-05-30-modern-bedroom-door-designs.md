## 3. Flush Slab Door: The Cleanest Modern Look

### 3.1 Step-by-Step: Install a Flush Hollow-Core Door

A flush slab door is completely flat with no panels or moulding. It's the most minimalist option and works perfectly in modern, Scandinavian, or Japandi-style bedrooms.

**What you need:**
- Pre-hung hollow-core flush door (Masonite 24"×80" Interior Door, ~$80–$130 at Home Depot)
- Hammer, pry bar, and chisel
- Screwdriver and drill
- Door shims (6-pack, ~$4 at Lowe's)
- New hinge set (3-pack brushed nickel hinges, ~$12–$18)
- Door lever handle set (Baldwin Prestige, ~$45–$80 at Home Depot)

**Steps:**

1. Remove the old door by unscrewing the hinges. Have someone hold the door while you pull the last hinge pin.
2. Pry off the old door casing trim carefully with a pry bar if you're replacing the whole frame.
3. If reusing the existing frame, hold the new flush door against the opening and check the fit — you want a 1/8-inch gap on all sides.
4. Mark and chisel out mortises for the new hinges on both the door and the frame.
5. Attach hinges to the door first, then to the frame. Use a level to check the door hangs straight.
6. Insert shims at the hinge side if the door sags — tap them in until the gap is even.
7. Mark and drill the latch hole using the template that comes with your handle set.
8. Install the handle set and test the latch — it should click smoothly without binding.

**Cost estimate:** $150–$250 total for door, hardware, and basic tools.

## 4. Shaker Panel Door: The Most Versatile Modern Style

The shaker door has one or more recessed rectangular panels. It works in modern farmhouse, transitional, and classic contemporary bedrooms — it's the best all-rounder.

### 4.1 How to Choose the Right Shaker Door

**1-panel vs 2-panel vs 5-panel:**

| Panel Count | Look | Best Room Style |
|-------------|------|----------------|
| 1 panel | Very modern, clean | Minimalist, contemporary |
| 2 panel | Balanced, versatile | Modern farmhouse, transitional |
| 5 panel | Traditional feel | Classic, craftsman homes |

**Steps:**

1. Measure your rough opening (the hole in the wall) — width and height.
2. Buy a pre-hung door sized 2 inches smaller than the rough opening (standard is 80"×32" or 80"×36").
3. Remove the old door and frame by cutting any caulk lines with a utility knife and prying the jamb away.
4. Set the new pre-hung door unit into the opening. Check plumb (vertical) and level (horizontal) with a long level.
5. Shim behind all three hinges and at the top corners until the door is perfectly plumb and level.
6. Nail through the jamb and shims into the wall studs using 2½-inch finish nails.
7. Trim the shims flush with a utility knife.
8. Install casing trim around the door on both sides.
9. Paint or stain the door before installing hardware.

## 5. Sliding Barn Door: Bold, Space-Saving, and Trendy

### 5.1 Step-by-Step: Install a Sliding Barn Door

A sliding barn door doesn't need a door swing clearance — it slides along the wall. This frees up floor space and creates an instant focal point in any bedroom.

**What you need:**
- Barn door slab (~$150–$400; Belleze Solid Wood Barn Door on Amazon, or Home Depot HDX series)
- Barn door hardware kit — track, rollers, floor guide (Homedepot.com sliding door hardware kit, ~$80–$150)
- Stud finder
- Drill and drill bits
- Level
- Lag screws (included with most hardware kits)

**Steps:**

1. Measure your door opening width. Your barn door should be at least 4 inches wider than the opening on each side (e.g., 36" opening needs a 40"–44" door).
2. Find wall studs using a stud finder. Mark them with tape — your track header board must attach to studs.
3. Cut or buy a 2×6 header board the same length as your track. Paint or stain it to match the door.
4. Mount the header board to the wall studs at the correct height (door height + 1 inch above the opening + track height).
5. Attach the track to the header board using the lag screws provided — check it's level before tightening fully.
6. Hang the door rollers onto the track and test sliding — the door should glide smoothly.
7. Install the floor guide at the bottom centre of the door opening to keep the door from swinging outward.
8. Install a door pull handle (no knob needed) — a simple J-pull or bar pull in matte black looks best.

[![Black sliding barn door on a wall-mounted track installed in a modern white bedroom hallway]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/home-decor/sliding-barn-door-bedroom-installation.jpg)](https://pin.it/pTFhbMNTz)

**Privacy note:** Barn doors leave a small gap at the edges. If privacy matters, add a door sweep at the bottom and magnetic catches on the door edge to reduce gaps.

**Cost estimate:** $280–$600 complete (door + hardware + installation).

## 6. Frosted Glass Panel Door: Light Without Sacrifice

A frosted glass insert lets light pass between rooms while maintaining privacy. It works beautifully in modern, Scandinavian, and transitional bedrooms — especially in darker hallways.

### 6.1 Step-by-Step: Install a Frosted Glass Bedroom Door

**Steps:**

1. Buy a pre-hung door with a frosted glass lite already installed (JELD-WEN Craftsman 1-Lite Frosted Glass Door, ~$250–$450 at Home Depot or Lowe's).
2. Remove the existing door and frame as described in Section 3.1.
3. Set the pre-hung unit in the opening and check plumb and level carefully — glass doors are heavier than hollow-core and need solid shimming.
4. Shim at all three hinge points, the latch side, and the top.
5. Drive 3-inch screws through the hinge jamb into the wall studs at shim locations.
6. Install the casing trim on both sides.
7. For the handle, choose a passage set with a privacy lock (no key needed — just a push-button lock from inside).

## 7. Black-Framed Glass Door: The Statement Door

The black steel-framed glass door is everywhere in high-end interior design right now. It creates a dramatic, editorial look and works in modern, industrial, and transitional bedrooms.

### 7.1 Step-by-Step: Add Black Frame Trim to an Existing Glass Door

If a full door replacement isn't in your budget, you can get a similar look by adding black metal trim tape or stick-on frame dividers to an existing clear glass door.

**Steps:**

1. Clean the glass surface thoroughly with glass cleaner — any dust or grease will prevent adhesion.
2. Buy self-adhesive black metal tape (¾-inch wide, Frost King or similar, ~$8–$15 on Amazon).
3. Measure and cut tape strips to create a grid or cross-bar pattern on the glass.
4. Apply strips vertically first — use a level to ensure they're straight.
5. Apply horizontal strips across, pressing firmly at intersections.
6. Use a small roller or credit card to push out any air bubbles.
7. Paint the door frame matte black to complete the look (Rust-Oleum Matte Black Spray, ~$7–$10 at Home Depot).

## 8. Door Hardware: The Finishing Touch

Even the most basic door looks stunning with the right hardware. Changing just the handle, hinges, and strike plate is the cheapest upgrade on this list.

### 8.1 Step-by-Step: Swap Old Hardware for Modern Handles

**Steps:**

1. Remove the old handle set by unscrewing the two screws on the interior rosette — the whole assembly pulls apart.
2. Remove the old latch by unscrewing two screws on the latch plate (on the door edge).
3. Check the bore hole diameter on your door — standard is 2⅛ inches. Most new handles fit this.
4. Slide the new latch into the edge hole and screw the latch plate flush.
5. Push both sides of the handle through the bore hole so the spindle bar connects inside.
6. Screw the interior side's two screws down firmly — don't overtighten or the handle will bind.
7. Replace hinges: unscrew one hinge at a time and replace with matching new hinges (same size) to avoid re-mortising.

## 9. Paint Your Bedroom Door: Biggest Impact, Lowest Cost

### 9.1 Step-by-Step: Paint a Bedroom Door Like a Pro

Painting a door a bold or contrasting colour is the fastest way to make it look modern — no replacement needed.

**Steps:**

1. Remove the door from its hinges (easier to paint flat — lean it against a wall or lay on sawhorses).
2. Lightly sand the entire door with 120-grit sandpaper to scuff the old finish.
3. Wipe with a tack cloth to remove all dust.
4. Apply a coat of primer (Zinsser Bulls Eye 1-2-3, ~$15–$20 at Home Depot) — this is especially important if painting over a dark colour or glossy surface.
5. Let primer dry fully (1–2 hours).
6. Apply the first coat of door paint using a 4-inch foam roller for flat areas and a 2-inch brush for panel edges and grooves.
7. Let dry 2–4 hours, lightly sand with 220-grit, wipe clean.
8. Apply second coat — this coat should look smooth and even.
9. Let cure for 24 hours before rehinging and 72 hours before full hardening.

## 10. Door Moulding Upgrade: Add Detail to a Flat Door

### 10.1 Step-by-Step: Add Shaker-Style Moulding to a Flat Door

You can turn a boring flat hollow-core door into a shaker-style door using thin MDF moulding strips — no new door needed.

**Steps:**

1. Buy thin MDF lattice strips or door moulding (1½-inch wide, ¼-inch thick — available at Home Depot in 8-ft lengths, ~$3–$6 per strip).
2. Paint the strips and door the same colour first (easier than painting around installed moulding).
3. Measure and mark the panel layout on the door face using a pencil and tape. A single large rectangle centred on the door is the most modern look.
4. Cut the moulding strips to length with a mitre saw or hand saw with a mitre box (cut corners at 45 degrees for clean joins).
5. Apply wood glue to the back of each strip and press firmly into position.
6. Secure with a pin nailer or small finish nails, sinking them slightly below the surface.
7. Fill nail holes with paintable wood filler, let dry, sand smooth.
8. Touch up the paint over all joins and nail holes.

## 12. FAQ

**Q: Can I replace a bedroom door myself without a contractor?**
Yes — installing a pre-hung door is a beginner-to-intermediate DIY project. Barn doors and basic hinged doors are the easiest. Pocket doors and pivot doors are best left to a professional.

**Q: What's the standard bedroom door size in the USA?**
The most common sizes are 80"×30", 80"×32", and 80"×36". Measure your existing rough opening before buying.

**Q: Should a bedroom door swing in or out?**
Almost always swing in (into the bedroom). Outward-swinging doors can hit people in the hallway and are usually only used in bathrooms or closets.

**Q: What's the best door material for soundproofing?**
A solid-core wood door is significantly better for sound than hollow-core. Pair it with a door sweep at the bottom and acoustic weatherstripping around the frame for the best results.

**Q: What colour door hardware is most popular in 2026?**
Matte black is the top trend for modern bedrooms. Brushed gold is second for warm, transitional spaces. Brushed nickel remains the most neutral and widely compatible finish.
